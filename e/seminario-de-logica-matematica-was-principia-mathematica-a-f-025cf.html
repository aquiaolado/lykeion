Seminário de Lógica Matemática "Was Principia Mathematica a failure? Alonzo Church as historian of logic: Frege, Russell, and Ramsey"

Ver na agenda oficial

Um seminário promovido pelo Centro de Estudos Matemáticos de Ciências ULisboa ( CEMS.UL ), com a participação de Bernard Linsky (Professor Emeritus, University of Alberta).

Abstract: Logicism is project of deriving mathematics from logical principles logic alone. It was started with Gottlob Frege in 1879 with his Begriffsschrift and survived Russell's paradox, but is thought to have ended with Bertrand Russell in 1910 with Principia Mathematica. The complaint, often attributed to Frank Ramsey, is that PM relied on the axioms of infinity, choice, and reducibility, none of which are logical truths. Instead, it was thought, foundations of mathematics should be conducted within set theory, an extensional, axiomatic, first order theory. So logicism gave way to set theory. Alonzo Church, in addition to being a great logician, was also an historian of logic. This talk will discuss some of Church's views on Frege, Russell and Ramsey. There is much of value in Church's discussion of Frege in the first chapter of Introduction to Mathematical Logic, and in his 1976 "Comparison of Russell's resolution of the semantic antinomies with that of Tarski". A link between his views can be found in his 1932 review of Frank Ramsey's “The Foundations of Mathematics” in1925. In particular Church's intensional logic and logic of sense and denotation can be seen as a long-delayed response to Ramsey's defense of simple type theory and a more successful version of logicism than many think.
